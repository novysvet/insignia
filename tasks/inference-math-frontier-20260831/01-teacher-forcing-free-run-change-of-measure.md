# Problem 1: From teacher-forced metrics to free-running failure

Repository: https://github.com/novysvet/insignia  
Reference branch/commit: `codex/glm53-dflash2-4070-super` at `0740c63`

## Why this matters

Insignia permits an approximate inference policy only when same-prefix
perplexity rises by at most 3.5% and difficult free-running answers remain
useful. Same-prefix evaluation is cheap and precise, but it evaluates the
candidate on histories generated by the exact model. During free generation,
one changed token moves the candidate onto a different history distribution.
The project already has examples where excellent local cosine or perplexity
does not prevent later route or answer divergence. A rigorous bridge—or a
rigorous impossibility theorem—would determine how much teacher-forced testing
is actually informative.

No GPU, model weights, or private traces are required. Work on a finite
vocabulary and synthetic Markov/autoregressive models using ordinary CPU time.

## Formal setup

Let `V` be a finite vocabulary and let `P_t(. | h)` and `Q_t(. | h)` be the
exact and approximate next-token laws for histories `h in V^t`. Let `P^T` and
`Q^T` be their induced laws on length-`T` trajectories. Greedy decoding may be
treated separately as the zero-temperature limit; do not silently apply a
sampling theorem to greedy argmax.

A teacher-forced corpus supplies histories sampled from an occupancy measure
`mu`, usually close to `P`, and observations such as

`ell(h) = KL(P_t(.|h) || Q_t(.|h))`, Jensen--Shannon divergence, Top-1
agreement, logit cosine, and the margin between the first two exact logits.

Let `F: V^T -> [0,1]` be a bounded failure functional. Examples include a
format failure, entering an absorbing nonsense state, changing a Lean proof
term before it closes, or exceeding a cumulative semantic-loss budget. Let
`tau` be the first failure time, possibly infinite.

## Main problem

Find the strongest useful relationship between teacher-forced local evidence
and `E_{Q^T}[F]` or `Q(tau <= T)`. Your result must explicitly handle the fact
that the measured history law and the deployed history law differ.

At minimum, do all of the following:

1. Prove the exact chain rule for trajectory KL and derive at least one stopped
   likelihood-ratio or change-of-measure bound valid for adaptive stopping
   times.
2. Determine when a bound based only on
   `E_{h~P}[sum_t ell(h_t)]` is minimax tight, and construct finite-state
   counterexamples showing when it is useless despite arbitrarily small mean
   teacher-forced KL or perplexity delta.
3. Add one extra measurable quantity—examples are a density-ratio cap, a
   margin-tail condition, contraction of the history kernel, a Rényi-divergence
   bound, or an on-policy mixture sample—and prove exactly what it buys.
4. Treat greedy decoding separately. Characterize the weakest margin/error
   condition that guarantees identical greedy tokens for `T` steps, then show
   why average cosine or average MSE cannot provide it.
5. Design a sequential data-collection rule that mixes exact-policy,
   candidate-policy, and intervention histories and returns an upper confidence
   bound on free-run failure. It must remain valid when data collection stops
   early.

## Hard extension

Suppose the approximation controller observes a causal state `z_t` and chooses
an action `a_t` (expert count, precision, draft length, or exact fallback).
Actions alter both `Q_t` and future states. Derive a bound or impossibility
result for a policy selected from the same logged data used to certify it.
Account for selection bias; a post-hoc empirical average is not a certificate.

## Required CPU artifact

Implement a finite-state adversary generator and an exact dynamic-programming
oracle. For vocabularies of size 2--8 and horizons up to at least 50, it must:

- compute `P^T`, `Q^T`, trajectory KL, failure probability, and proposed bounds;
- search for high-failure pairs under a fixed teacher-forced KL budget;
- reproduce a rare-history cascade in which local average metrics look safe;
- test coverage of the proposed anytime confidence sequence.

Use exact rational arithmetic for at least the smallest cases so numerical
underflow cannot masquerade as a theorem.

## Acceptance and kill criteria

A useful positive result gives a computable certificate whose width is
non-vacuous at GLM-like horizons and whose assumptions correspond to quantities
the engine can log. A useful negative result proves that no such certificate is
possible from the existing teacher-forced fields, and identifies the minimum
additional on-policy information required. Kill any proposal that merely
restates Pinsker with a horizon-linear bound that exceeds one on all realistic
inputs.
